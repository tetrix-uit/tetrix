# Slop Patterns for Chat

Each pattern shows the fix. Examples are chat-shaped, not essay-shaped.

## 1. Binary contrasts

**Bad:** It is not X. It is Y.
**Fix:** State Y directly.
**Example:** "The question is not the model. It is the eval." becomes
"The eval matters more than the model."

## 2. Throat-clearing openers

**Bad:** Here is the thing, Here is what I mean, Let me be clear,
I will be honest, The uncomfortable truth is.
**Fix:** Cut the opener. State the point.

## 3. Faux-insight setups

**Bad:** What most people get wrong, Here is what nobody tells you,
The part everyone misses.
**Fix:** Cut the setup. Let the claim stand on its own.

## 4. Colon reveals

**Bad:** A noun phrase, a colon, then a lowercase dramatic reveal.
"The best part: it learns."
**Fix:** Write a plain sentence. "It learns from every run." Use colons
only for lists, labels, and quotes.

## 5. Importance puffery

**Bad:** marks a pivotal moment, stands as a testament, plays a vital
role, solidifies its position, underscores its significance.
**Fix:** State the fact. Let the reader judge. "The launch marks a
pivotal moment" becomes "The launch is the first paid product."

## 6. Weasel attribution

**Bad:** Experts agree, Studies show, Many argue, Widely regarded as.
**Fix:** Name the source or cut the claim. If there is no source, ask
instead of inventing one.

## 7. Synonym cycling

**Bad:** The agent reviews the draft. The assistant scores the piece.
The tool suggests fixes.
**Fix:** Repeat the right word. "The agent reviews the draft, scores it,
and suggests fixes."

## 8. Fake-profound kickers and summary recaps

**Bad:** A final deep line, a cute metaphor, or a restatement:
"In conclusion", "Ultimately", "Overall".
**Fix:** Delete the kicker. End on the last concrete point or the next
action. Do not rewrite the metaphor into a better metaphor.

## 9. Formatting slop

**Bad:** Emoji in headings, bold sprinkled mid-sentence for emphasis,
a bullet list where two sentences read better, a header over a
two-sentence section.
**Fix:** Format follows content. Prose by default. Lists only for real
lists: steps, options, or findings.

## 10. Em dashes

**Bad:** A dash cluster used as default rhythm.
**Fix:** Use none in short replies. Use 1-2 in a long reply only when a
comma or a period reads worse. Remove decorative dashes.

## The two tests

- **Portability test.** If a sentence fits another product, company, or
  team unchanged, it is filler. Cut it or add a fact specific to this
  subject: a name, a number, a path, a mechanism, a consequence.
- **Show-don-tell.** Cut lines that label a point important, surprising,
  or obvious: The key point is, As you can see, In other words. Give the
  fact and trust the reader.
